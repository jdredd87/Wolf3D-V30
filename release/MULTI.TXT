WOLFENSTEIN 3-D, MULTIPLAYER  --  by StevenC and Claude (Anthropic)
==================================================================
Version 1.11.1, 2026-10-23

id Software's Wolfenstein 3-D for up to four players over a network:
deathmatch, team deathmatch, capture the flag or co-op, each with or
without enemies, DOOM's rules (no
lives, SPACE to respawn, frags, items back after a while, in co-op one
player's keys are everyone's).  Machines of any speed play together --
an 8 MHz NEC V30 and a Pentium II in one game -- because every machine
plays every step of the same game: a slow one draws fewer pictures, not
a different game.

Tested: eight hours at a time on a NEC V30, a 486 and a Pentium II with a
server bot, every one of the sixty floors in co-op with enemies, every
deathmatch floor -- no machine ever disagreed with another.

New in 1.4: the server runs on DOS too, and on Windows without Python --
MPSRV.EXE and MPSRVW.EXE, one Free Pascal source.  Eight hours with MPSRV
on the Pentium II serving the 486 and the V30: no machine disagreed once.
Each DOS machine has served a game in turn -- the 8 MHz V30 too -- and
MPSRVW has served all three at once, without a desync.

1.4.1: MPSRV keeps up better on a slow machine -- it writes its log once a
second, and has room for twice as many packets waiting.
1.4.2: MPSRV /? and MPSRVW /? explain every option, with examples.
1.4.3: SETUP.TXT, the setup guide.
1.5: SETUP.TXT has the server's console in full -- every command, with
an example; on a plain 8086 or 8088 the PLAY menu says the network game
needs a V20/V30 or better (WOLF3DM uses 80186 instructions).  The game
and the servers are the same as 1.4.3's.
1.6: the free SHAREWARE episode plays too -- WOLF3DM1.EXE, on the
shareware 1.4's *.WL1 files (episode 1, ten floors): anyone can try a
network game without buying anything.  Every server now waits 1 ms after
each packet by default (--txgap; MPSRV always did): a PicoMEM machine
joining late could not catch up from the Windows or Python server.
1.8: FIND -- the game lists the servers on your network and you pick one,
no address to type.  WATCH -- up to four people watch a game as well as
four play, through any player's eyes (1 to 4).  --record GAME.MPR on any
server writes the whole game, every floor, and WOLF3DM REPLAY GAME.MPR
plays it back on any machine (F for fast).  Two team modes, Gold (players
1 and 3) against Silver (2 and 4): team deathmatch, and capture the flag.
The build check works (it never did): a game only joins a server running
the same version and the same data.  All of it still fits in an 8 MHz
V30's memory: the network game leaves out the debug keys, the help
screens and the profiler, which it never used.
1.8.1: a slow machine joining late can always catch up.  "Catching up"
shows the real number of steps to go (it showed only those already
received, so a V30 closing the gap looked as if it never would) and how
long is left; and if a joiner would take more than a minute, everyone
waits for it -- "Waiting for NAME to catch up" -- until the rest can be
closed with the game running (--joinwait SECS on the server; 0: never).
1.9: a late joiner starts where the game is.  The server asks a machine
already playing for a snapshot of the game -- everything in it, 47 KB --
and the joiner ("Getting the game") starts from it instead of replaying
the game from its start: an 8 MHz V30 joining an hour-old game is in
within half a minute.  Proved exact: a game started from a snapshot plays
on with the same checksums, step for step, as one that never stopped.
--nosnap on a server turns it off.  WOLF3DM leaves out the single-player
intermission and save game, which it never used, for the V30's memory.
1.9.1: the text over the view -- notices, the scores (TAB), "Killed by",
chat -- drawn in assembly: on an 8 MHz V30 the picture with text up went
from 11.5 to 13.2 frames a second.
1.9.2: LOWEST's walls drawn a little faster (the copy of each cast
column's height in assembly): 1.8% on an 8 MHz V30.
1.10.1: no more black squares over the start-up screen (id's memory
and sound boxes) while "Joining the server" shows.
1.10.2: a late joiner started from a snapshot on a floor where a
pushwall had moved fell out of step with the others: the snapshot now
carries the map itself, which a pushwall changes.
1.10.3: NETBOT, the game playing itself, only plays: its pretend deaths,
scores and "here, step N" chat lines -- for testing on camera -- are
NETBOT SHOW now.  Played across the Internet: a DOSBox-X PC elsewhere
against two DOS machines, the server on one of them (SETUP.TXT).
1.11: SPEAR OF DESTINY on the network -- SPEARM.EXE on its *.SOD, all 21
floors in id's order (the secret floors, and the Spear taking everyone to
the Angel of Death), on all three servers.  And WINDOWS: W3DWIN.ZIP has
the whole game for Windows 95 and later -- registered, shareware and
Spear -- and Windows and DOS machines play one network game together.
The game's checksum changed so that a Windows machine can compute it too,
so 1.11 and 1.10 do not play together: put 1.11 on every machine.  On an
8 MHz V30 whose PicoMEM card plays the Sound Blaster, Spear froze the
machine now and then: play Spear there with NOSB (SETUP.TXT).
1.11.1: MKBJ.EXE -- run once in the game's folder, it makes the other
players BJ in four colours (grey, green, red, brown) instead of the SS,
from your own data: VSWAPM.WL6, .WL1 or .SOD beside your VSWAP (MKBJW.EXE
on Windows, in W3DWIN.ZIP).  Every packet the DOS game sends now carries a
UDP checksum, and a damaged one it is sent is dropped: a PicoMEM 2 garbles
a frame sent straight after another, and a server took the garbage for a
player's packet.  The text over the view -- notices, chat, "Killed by" --
drawn in assembly: an 8 MHz V30 draws 7% more pictures a second with text
up.  1.11.1 and 1.11 play together.

SETUP.TXT walks through setting it all up, step by step -- the network
on each DOS machine, the game, the server, joining, and troubleshooting.

WHAT YOU NEED
-------------
* On every player's DOS machine: Wolfenstein 3-D 1.4's data -- not
  included, it is id's -- either the REGISTERED *.WL6 files (the game is
  WOLF3DM.EXE) or the free SHAREWARE *.WL1 files (WOLF3DM1.EXE; the
  shareware is 1WOLF14.ZIP, on any DOS game archive).  Everyone in a game
  uses the same one.  And a V20/V30, an 80186 or anything from a 286 up,
  VGA, DOS.
* A network card with a packet driver loaded (any Ethernet card's, at a
  software interrupt from 60h to 80h -- the game finds it), and the
  machine's address in a text file the game reads: the one %MTCPCFG%
  names (mTCP's), or C:\AI\NET.CFG (DOS Bridge's):

      IPADDR 192.168.1.20
      NETMASK 255.255.255.0
      GATEWAY 192.168.1.1

  (PACKETINT 0x60 says which interrupt, if the search should not.)
* One machine on the same network to run the server -- any of three:
  a DOS machine with a packet driver (MPSRV.EXE), a Windows PC
  (MPSRVW.EXE), or any PC with Python 3 (mpserver.py).  A PC's firewall
  must let in UDP port 31992 from the local network.


INSTALLING
----------
On each DOS machine: copy WOLF3DM.EXE, W3MENU.EXE and PLAY.BAT into the
directory with your *.WL6 files (WOLF3DM1.EXE instead of WOLF3DM.EXE, into
the one with the shareware's *.WL1).  (W3MENU.EXE and PLAY.BAT are the same
menu as the base package's, with a network page added; they replace it.)

For the server: MPSRV.EXE onto the DOS machine that will host, or the
SERVER directory onto the PC.


HOSTING
-------
Any one of the three -- the same switches, the same console:

    MPSRV --players 4 --mode dm --noenemies --itemrespawn 30          (DOS)
    SERVER\MPSRVW --players 4 --bots 1 --mode coop --skill 2 --map 0  (Windows)
    python SERVER\mp\mpserver.py --players 4 --mode dm --noenemies   (Python)

MPSRV /? (or MPSRVW /?) explains every option, with its default, and gives
examples -- on DOS, MPSRV /? | MORE shows it a page at a time.

    --players N       how many people (up to 4, bots included)
    --mode M          coop, dm (deathmatch), tdm (team deathmatch) or
                      ctf (capture the flag) -- TEAMS below
    --noenemies       an empty map
    --ff off          no friendly fire in co-op
    --fraglimit N     deathmatch: a floor ends at N frags
    --timelimit MIN   a floor ends after MIN minutes
    --itemrespawn S   items come back after S seconds
    --bots N          the server plays N of the slots itself
    --map N           start on map N (0-59: episode*10 + floor-1)
    --skill 0-3       as the game's own difficulty
    --record F.MPR    record the whole game, for WOLF3DM REPLAY F.MPR
    --joinwait S      everyone waits for a joiner who would take more than
                      S seconds to catch up (60; 0: never)
    --nosnap          joiners replay the game from its start, not from a
                      snapshot of it
    --name NAME       the name FIND lists this server by

The game starts when the first player has loaded the level.

While it runs, the server takes commands -- status, scores, say TEXT,
kick N reason, ban N, pause, resume, map E L, nextfloor, timelimit MIN,
fraglimit N, next ..., end, quit (help lists them) -- typed into it, or
sent from a PC:

    python SERVER\mp\mpctl.py status                     (on the same PC)
    python SERVER\mp\mpctl.py --host 192.168.1.30 status (to MPSRV on DOS)

MPSRV on DOS: the same packet driver and address file as the game.  It
runs the PC's timer at 70 Hz while it serves, as the game does, and puts
it back however it stops; it keeps the game's history in XMS (or in
MPSRV.HIS on the disk without it), so a machine can join hours into a
game.  A line at the bottom of its screen moves with the clock.  A DOS
machine that serves cannot also play.  --txgap 1000 (the default) waits a
millisecond after each packet it sends: a PicoMEM card's WiFi garbles one
that follows another too closely.

MPSRVW on Windows: the console is its window (the status is in the title).
If Windows asks whether to let it on the network, allow it -- a question
left unanswered makes a firewall rule that blocks it, and that rule wins
over any rule for the port.


JOINING
-------
    WOLF3DM NET 192.168.1.10 NAME STEVE

or PLAY and the menu's network page (M): S the server's address, Y your
name, J to join.  Both are remembered in WOLF3DM.CFG, so after the first
time WOLF3DM NET alone is enough.

    T      chat                 TAB    the scores
    ESC    asks, then leaves    SPACE  respawn, when dead

Anyone can join a game already running: the new machine replays the game
from a snapshot a machine already playing gives it ("Getting the game"),
plays the last few seconds fast ("Catching up: N steps to go", and how
long is left) and walks in -- an 8 MHz V30 joins an hour-old game in under
half a minute.  If a joiner would still take more than a minute, everyone
waits for it ("Waiting for NAME to catch up") until the rest can be
closed with the game going.  A network dropout of up to a minute is
ridden out the same way.

On a slow machine, LOWEST (or the menu's A) draws the least picture:
    WOLF3DM NET LOWEST VIEW 10

Do not know the server's address?

    WOLF3DM NET FIND        (or F on the menu's network page)

lists every server on your network -- its name, its game, who is in it --
and you press its number.


WATCHING AND REPLAYING
----------------------
    WOLF3DM NET 192.168.1.10 WATCH      (or W on the network page)

watches instead of playing: 1 to 4 picks whose eyes you see through.  Up
to four can watch as well as four play, and a watcher changes nothing in
anyone's game.

A server started with --record MATCH.MPR records the whole game -- every
floor, the names, the chat.  On any machine with the game:

    WOLF3DM REPLAY MATCH.MPR

F runs it eight times as fast (F again: normal), 1 to 4 changes the view,
ESC stops.


TEAMS
-----
--mode tdm and --mode ctf put players 1 and 3 on GOLD and 2 and 4 on
SILVER, each team in its colour.  Teammates cannot hurt each other.

Team deathmatch: a team's score is its players' frags added up, shown on
top of the scores (TAB); --fraglimit is a team's.

Capture the flag: Gold's flag is the gold key, at player 1's start;
Silver's is the silver key, at player 2's.  Walk onto the other team's
flag to take it; carry it to your own flag, at home, to score.  A carrier
who dies drops it; touch your own dropped flag to send it home, or it
goes home by itself after 30 seconds.  --fraglimit counts captures.


IF A MACHINE WITH A PICOMEM CARD LOCKS UP
-----------------------------------------
On our NEC V30, whose AdLib is a PicoMEM 2 card's emulated one (firmware
of 2026-09-27), the machine froze or lost its WiFi every few hours of
network play; with the AdLib switched off it ran eight hours clean.  If
yours does the same, add NOAL (the effects then play on the PC speaker):

    WOLF3DM NET NOAL

Machines with a real AdLib or Sound Blaster chip were never affected.
